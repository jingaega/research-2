## 1. Environment and data starting point

- **R was not installed** at session start. I installed it from Ubuntu apt and Bioconductor and checked it
  with `packageVersion()`: R 4.3.3, limma 3.58.1, affy 1.80.0, GEOquery 2.70.0, frma 1.54.0,
  oligo 1.66.0.
- Python 3.11.15, scikit-learn 1.9.1, numpy 2.4.6, pandas 3.0.6, scipy 1.17.1, torch 2.14.1 (CPU),
  neuroCombat. Hardware: 4 CPUs, 15 GB RAM.
- **Starting point: raw CEL files with frozen RMA (fRMA).** Every series retained for modeling is
  Affymetrix (HG-U133A, HG-U133 Plus 2, HuGene 1.0 ST), so no non-Affymetrix normalisation is needed.
  - fRMA was chosen over RMA because it normalises each array against frozen external vectors. No
    information passes between arrays, so preprocessing cannot leak across folds or into the test set
    (rule 4). Ordinary RMA would quantile-normalise test arrays with training arrays.
  - The deposited series matrices were used only for a first QC pass and then abandoned.
    GSE12649's deposit is per-gene median-normalised. The Sibille deposits carry no usable sex signal,
    whereas the same CELs under fRMA give 0/108 sex mismatches.
- **Probe → gene mapping:** Bioconductor annotation packages. Probesets that hit more than one Entrez
  gene are dropped, and the remaining probesets are averaged per gene (a fixed rule). For GSE92538 the
  U133 Plus 2 arrays are restricted to U133A probesets.
- **Cross-platform intersection:** 11,972 Entrez genes are on all modeling platforms. Dropping chrY genes
  and XIST/TSIX leaves **11,955 features**, so a classifier cannot use donor sex composition.

## 5. Development evaluation schemes

- **(a) Repeated donor-grouped CV:** `StratifiedGroupKFold(5, shuffle=True, random_state=r)`,
  r = 0..4, so 25 folds per task. Stratified by diagnosis|cohort, grouped by split group. The same
  folds are used for every model.
- **(b) Leave-one-cohort-out** (the "study" unit is the cohort): hold out each cohort that
  contributes to the task, one at a time. Results are descriptive and per cohort, with no significance
  tests (rule 10).

## 6. Keeping batch correction and everything else in-fold

Everything data-dependent is fitted on the training part of each fold only: batch-adjustment
parameters, feature filters, co-expression graphs, pathway z-scores and hyperparameters (inner 3-fold
CV on the training part).

- **Baseline adjustment: per-batch standardisation.** Per gene, subtract the batch mean and divide by
  the batch SD, both estimated from that batch's **training** donors, then apply to its held-out donors.
  - This is label-free. It is one of the "batch-by-batch" methods that Hornung et al. (2017) note need
    no modification for add-on use.
  - Every batch appears in every training fold (the folds are stratified by cohort), and in training
    for the locked test set.
- **Unseen cohort (leave-one-cohort-out):** its batches are standardised with their **own** means and
  SDs, computed from all held-out donors together, **without labels**.
  - This is label-free and transductive: it needs the held-out cohort as a batch.
  - Caveat: it centres the cohort on its own class mix. That removes the between-cohort part of the
    disease signal, but not the within-cohort contrast, and it is the same for every model.
- **ComBat variant (V5):** neuroCombat with diagnosis as a protected covariate, fitted on training
  donors only. Held-out donors are adjusted with the training-estimated batch parameters, which needs
  no labels. For an unseen cohort, ComBat cannot estimate the batch, so it falls back to label-free
  self-standardisation.
- Diagnosis labels of held-out donors are never used by any adjustment.

## 8. Statistics and interpretation

- **Primary metric:** macro-F1. Also reported: MCC, per-class precision and recall, confusion
  matrices (summed over the 25 folds, and per fold in the JSON), AUC (one-vs-rest macro for Task B), and
  the chance levels next to every headline number.
- **Model comparisons:** the Nadeau–Bengio corrected resampled t-test on the **25 fold-level
  differences** (never repetition averages), df = 24, with the uncorrected p reported beside it.
  - Assumption: the correlation between fold estimates induced by overlapping training sets is
    approximated by n_test/n_train (≈ 0.25). This is a heuristic, not an unbiased variance estimate.
  - No switching of tests, and no Wilcoxon test on 5 folds.
  - Graph comparisons are reported per task. A Holm-adjusted p across the family of graph tests
    (variants × tasks) is given alongside the raw p.
- **Seed vs fold variance:**
  - Fold variance = the SD of the 25 per-fold means over seeds.
  - Seed variance = the mean within-fold SD across seeds (3 seeds for stochastic components: random
    graphs, RF, GCN).
  - Logistic regression is deterministic, so its seed variance is reported as 0.
- **Graph × signal strength** (the central question): Δ(graph − no-graph) and Δ(graph − random graph)
  per task, with corrected tests. Strong contrast = SCZ vs CTL; weak = BD/MDD vs CTL and Task B. The
  tasks are not paired on the same folds, so the difference between tasks is reported descriptively,
  with each task's corrected CI.
- **Rules applied:** gains below about 0.03, or gains not holding across folds, are not claimed. A model
  that does not clearly beat C1 (study-only), or collapses under leave-one-cohort-out, is reported as
  having learned study differences.

## 9. Final selection and the single test evaluation (rule 11)

- Chosen from dev CV only, frozen, then evaluated **once** on the locked test set, on all 4 tasks.
- **Best model** = the highest mean dev-CV macro-F1 averaged over the 4 tasks among non-baseline
  configurations (graph, pathway, ensemble, ComBat-augmented). This guarantees the graph question
  gets a test-set answer.
- **Best simple baseline** = the highest among {C0, V6-on-C0, V7}.
- Final training uses all dev donors of the task and the same fixed hyperparameter procedure (inner-CV
  C). Test batches are adjusted with training statistics of the same batch.
- **Two-track table** for each of the two configurations × 4 tasks: (1) locked-test macro-F1, MCC,
  AUC, per-class P/R and confusion matrix, with a **bootstrap 95% CI over test donors** and a statement
  of CI width given per-class test n (e.g. Task B has about 20 test donors per class, so the macro-F1
  CI is roughly ±0.1); (2) 5×5 dev CV mean ± SD over folds; (3) leave-one-cohort-out per held-out
  cohort.

## 11. Points I am escalating (rule 13)

1. **Rule 12 vs "train nothing before approval".** Timing a configuration requires fitting models. I
   did it on dev donors only, with **permuted labels**, and computed no metric, so no result exists.
2. **"Study" for the ≥3-study rule.** Counting GEO accessions would treat GSE12649 and GSE35978 as
   two BD studies, but they profile the same Stanley brains. I count **cohorts** (brain banks), which is
   stricter. BD and MDD pass with exactly 3. Adding GSE92538 is what made BD eligible, and I disclose
   that I knew this when adding it.
3. **Duplicate detection is incomplete** (validation sensitivity 0.42 at the probable tier). Merging
   same-bank series into cohorts and using liberal split groups is my mitigation. Residual undetected
   duplicates can only exist *within* a cohort, so leave-one-cohort-out is immune to them; the CV
   estimates could still be slightly optimistic.
4. **Primary vs sensitivity for sex mismatches.** The protocol asks to "rerun without the affected
   donors". I made the primary analysis exclude them, because their labels are unreliable. The
   sensitivity triad therefore uses "all eligible donors" as its base.
5. **Test-set size.** Some diagnosis × cohort test cells hold 3–4 donors. Per-cohort test numbers will
   not be interpretable, and I will report only task-level test metrics with CIs.
6. **Nadeau–Bengio with macro-F1.** The test is defined for a loss averaged over test points. Macro-F1
   is not such an average, so the corrected test is an approximation here. I will use it as the
   protocol requires, and I also report the fold-level AUC differences under the same test.

## 12. Changes from the expanded literature review (39 sources read; see LITERATURE.md)

1. **Optimism of the selected configuration (Varma & Simon 2006; Tsamardinos et al. 2018).** The dev-CV
   score of whichever configuration is chosen as "best" among ≤ 12 is optimistically biased even with
   nested tuning. I will additionally report the **bootstrap bias-corrected CV (BBC-CV)** estimate for
   the two selected configurations. It is computed from the stored out-of-fold predictions of all
   configurations, with no extra fits. The locked test set stays the only unbiased number.
2. **The graph variants use the SGC form (Wu et al. 2019):** fixed low-pass filter over the gene graph,
   then a linear classifier. The graph is then the only difference from the no-structure baseline.
   Equal tuning and repeated identical splits for all models follow Shchur et al. 2018 and Errica et al.
   2020.
3. **Cell-type composition (Hagenauer et al. 2018; Ramaker et al. 2017)** is the dominant axis of
   variation in these datasets. It shifts with diagnosis (neuron down, astrocyte up in SCZ/BD) but also
   with agonal hypoxia and dissection. Added as a **reserve variant candidate (V9): a classifier on
   cell-type marker scores only**, to measure how much of any signal is composition. It will be
   pre-registered in LOG.md before running, and counts against the budget.
4. **Per-batch standardisation stays the baseline adjustment (Luo et al. 2010).** Mean-centering and
   standardisation were equal to or better than no correction in 75–79% of MAQC-II cross-batch
   prediction cases. They are not always beneficial, which is what V5 (ComBat; Johnson et al. 2007)
   tests.
5. **Stated limitations, from the dataset papers themselves:**
   - Medication effects cannot be separated from disease (Iwamoto et al. 2005 attribute mitochondrial
     down-regulation mainly to medication).
   - SCZ signal depends on illness stage (Narayan et al. 2008).
   - Gene-level microarray features miss isoform-level effects, where the largest effects lie (Gandal
     et al. 2018b).
6. **Provenance caveat.** GEO does not name the brain bank for GSE21138, and I could not retrieve the
   paper's full text. The cohort key "Victoria" is a label from background knowledge, not a verified
   fact. The identity audit's negative controls assumed this cohort shares no donors with Pritzker. The
   highest negative-control hit (z = 5.08, which set the match threshold) was a GSE21138–GSE92538 pair.
   If that pair were a real shared donor, the threshold would be too conservative (fewer duplicates
   called), not too lax, and neither cohort is split across folds with the other (they are different
   cohorts). The pair is listed in the audit for transparency.
7. **Paper–GEO conflicts** (reported per protocol, in LITERATURE.md):
   - Gandal 2018's list contains non-brain GSE11223.
   - Chang 2014's subject counts differ from GEO's for GSE54568/GSE54572.
   - Lanz 2019's hippocampal results rest on arrays our audit finds mislabelled.
